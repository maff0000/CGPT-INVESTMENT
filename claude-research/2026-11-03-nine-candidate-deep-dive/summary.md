# Nine QARP candidates: cross-study summary — 2026-11-03

As of 2026-11-03, all three earlier date leads check out: **ADMA reports 2026-11-04 after the close, GMED 2026-11-05 after the close, and FERG 2026-11-09 before the open**. The other six names have already reported their latest quarter, and none of them has an announced next date. On the evidence gathered, the names that combine proven quality with a price below their own history most cleanly are **EMCOR (EME), Globus Medical (GMED), Zurn Elkay (ZWS) and PepsiCo (PEP)**, and each comes with a different catch. EME's 37% ROIC and net-cash balance sheet sit on top of a backlog that is now roughly half data-centre work. GMED's discount has to survive a print in two days. ZWS's multiple has compressed partly because its end markets are expected to shrink in 2027. PEP is cheap for reasons: guidance has been cut and North America is flat. Ferguson (FERG) and Mondelēz (MDLZ) look fairly priced rather than cheap. Align (ALGN) and ADMA are cheap but fail parts of the quality test: ALGN on growth, ADMA on concentration and governance. Ollie's (OLLI) is the weakest fit, because its headline Q2 beat came from a one-off tariff refund that was larger than its entire gross-margin gain. Tariff refunds also flatter PEP and ZWS, and AI/data-centre capex is the biggest shared exposure across the industrials. Everything below is research framing, not personal investment advice or a buy/sell recommendation. Bear/base/bull figures are illustrative arithmetic, not price targets.

## Industrials: data-centre money lifts EME and FERG, while ZWS prepares for shrinking markets

### EME: a 37% ROIC compounder whose backlog is now half data centres

EMCOR's Q3 2026, reported 2026-10-29, was among the strongest prints in this set **[VERIFIED]**. Revenue was a record **$5.54B, up 28.8% (organic +25.5%)**. Non-GAAP EPS was **$9.60, up 46%**, and the non-GAAP operating margin rose to 10.5% from 9.4%. Remaining performance obligations (RPOs) reached **$18.57B, up 47%**. FY26 guidance was raised for the third time, to EPS of $34.50–35.50; the original February guide was $27.25–29.25 ([Q3 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000105634/000010563426000132/eme-ex991_2026930xq3.htm); [Q4 2025 release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260226702092/en)). Over five years, operating margin roughly doubled from 5.4% (FY21) to 9.9% (TTM), FCF grew from $283M to $1.48B, ROIC has run between the high-30s and high-40s since FY23, and the share count fell 2% year on year **[VERIFIED]** ([stockanalysis financials](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eme/financials/); [ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eme/financials/ratios/)). The catch is concentration. Data-centre RPOs of **$8.74B are about 47% of backlog**, and data centres were about one-third of YTD revenue. Network & Communications revenue grew nearly 130% in Mechanical **[REPORTED]**, per call highlights ([MarketBeat, 2026-10-29](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-emcor-group-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-10-29/)). Management says it serves hyperscalers rather than speculative "neoclouds".

The market's response to all this was muted. The shares rose about 1.8% on the print **[REPORTED]** ([StockStory](https://stockstory.org/us/stocks/nyse/eme/news/earnings/emcors-nyseeme-q3-2026-strong-sales-full-year-sales-guidance-is-optimistic)), after an 8-session slide of 11.4% while the S&P fell only 0.7%. Through 2026-10-21, EME was −7.5% over one month against a flat S&P, and +0.1% over one year against +16.5% for the index **[REPORTED]** ([Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/stock/eme/articles/619901/emcor-stock-8-straight-red-days-down-11/2026-10-22)). The 52-week change is +4.0%, which trails FIX, PWR and MYRG by 30–40 points. At 17.9x forward and 14.0x EV/EBITDA, EME trades at a discount to its closest peer, Comfort Systems (FIX: 20.5x and 18.8x), but above its own FY21–25 year-end EV/EBITDA range of 10–15x **[VERIFIED]** ([FIX](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fix/statistics/)). Our reading is that the market is discounting the durability of the data-centre cycle, not doubting this year's numbers **[PROVISIONAL]**. Margin quality is mixed. Electrical margin rose 200 bp to 13.3%, Mechanical slipped to 12.1% on a shift to lower-markup prime-contractor and cost-plus work, Industrial Services earned just 1.8%, and a $34M impairment on a small energy-efficiency business was excluded from non-GAAP. FCF conversion above 100% in FY23–24 was likely helped by customer pre-billing during fast backlog growth, which reverses when growth slows **[PROVISIONAL]**.

**Thesis-breakers [PROVISIONAL]:** data-centre RPO falling below $8.74B, or total RPO declining sequentially, for two or more quarters; hyperscaler capex cuts or project deferrals cited on calls; operating margin below about 9%, or Electrical below about 11%; a full year of FCF below about 75% of net income; ROIC below about 25% as acquisitions accumulate; a move to sustained net debt; write-downs on large fixed-price jobs.

Globus is the healthcare name where quality is clearly improving. In Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-06; now nearly superseded), **sales rose 5.9% and about 9% excluding Nevro**, with US spine +7% and international spine +14%. The adjusted EBITDA margin was **35.4%, up from 28.0%**, non-GAAP EPS rose 56% to $1.34, and FY26 EPS guidance was raised to $4.95–5.05 while revenue guidance of $3.18–3.22B was only reaffirmed **[VERIFIED]** ([SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001237831/000162828026054353/gmed-q22026ex991.htm)). FCF has grown from about $104M (FY22) to **$756M TTM**, ROIC has recovered from 7.5% after the NuVasive deal to 13.9%, there are no borrowings, and the share count is down 1.2% **[VERIFIED]** ([stockanalysis ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/gmed/financials/ratios/); [stats](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/gmed/statistics/)). At 15.6x forward, 9.9x EV/EBITDA and 14.2x P/FCF, GMED trades below every year-end multiple from FY21 to FY25 (EV/EBITDA 13–24x). It has the best 52-week momentum in this set at +31.9%. Peer multiples (Stryker, Zimmer, Medtronic, ATEC) are **[UNKNOWN]**, so no "cheaper than peers" claim is made.

Three items are worth watching on 11-05. Enabling Technologies (robots) **fell about 26%** to $26.1M as customers moved to placements instead of purchases. **Thesis-breakers [PROVISIONAL]:** Q3 growth excluding Nevro below about 5%; a cut to FY26 revenue (below $3.18B) or EPS guidance; enabling-tech declines continuing without placement data; Nevro still falling by double digits; adjusted EBITDA margin moving back toward 30%; any controls issue or restatement tied to acquisition accounting; a large dilutive deal.

## Consumer defensive: cocoa relief, a PepsiCo reset and an Ollie's comp problem

### MDLZ: a cocoa tailwind, now largely priced in

Mondelēz's Q3, reported 2026-10-29, posted **organic growth of +2.1% led by volume** (vol/mix +1.8 pp, price +0.3 pp), the first time volume has outpaced price since late 2021 **[VERIFIED/REPORTED]**. Adjusted gross margin rose **380 bp** to 34.2% as cocoa deflation fed through, adjusted EPS was $0.78 (+5.5% constant currency), and FY guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001103982/000162828026068233/mdlzearningsreleasecontent.htm); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/mondelez-earnings-analysis-questions-answered-and-next-catalysts-93CH-4988419)). Cocoa is below $3,000/t, against the 2024 record of about $12,000 **[REPORTED]**. A projected 2026/27 deficit of about 190k t and El Niño risk are the swing factors **[REPORTED]** ([Yahoo/GuruFocus](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/mondelez-international-inc-mdlz-q3-071128774.html)). Emerging markets are the engine: AMEA organic +7.7%, with India and China growing above 20%. Europe was −2.1% organic and will see negative chocolate pricing for a couple of quarters. Quality is middling. ROIC has ranged from 6.1% to 10.8%, leverage is about 2.7–3.0x, and the 2025 trough (ROIC 6.1%, payout 101%, debt/EBITDA 3.8x) shows how badly cocoa can hit the business. The best feature is steady share-count reduction, −1.85% year on year **[VERIFIED]** ([stockanalysis ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mdlz/financials/ratios/)). The stock rose **12.3% over the month against SPY −2.0%** and sits just under its 52-week high, at 19.4x forward **[VERIFIED]** ([stockanalysis history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mdlz/history/)). Our reading is that the 2027 cocoa tailwind is partly priced in, so MDLZ is fair rather than cheap **[PROVISIONAL]**. Its hedge book is **[UNKNOWN]**.

**Thesis-breakers [PROVISIONAL]:** FY27 guidance (January) for constant-currency EPS growth below high single digits despite cocoa relief; vol/mix turning negative again; cocoa sustained well above about $5k/t; a large leveraged deal pushing debt/EBITDA above about 3.5x (the Hershey approach is a 2024 item with no sign of a 2026 renewal); EM growth slowing to low single digits.

### PEP: the highest-quality consumer franchise, cheap because it is under repair

PepsiCo's Q3, reported 2026-10-08, beat on revenue and EPS, with organic growth of +3.1%, the strongest since Q4 2023 **[VERIFIED/REPORTED]** ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000077476/000007747626000050/q320268-kxexhibit991.htm); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/pepsico-earnings-guidance-cramer.html)). Management conceded it is "not competing well in soft drinks" **[REPORTED]** ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/08/pepsico-pep-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Valuation is at a five-year low: 17.3x trailing against 22–31x in 2021–25, EV/EBITDA 11.9x against 13–19x, and a 4.3% dividend yield. That is cheaper than KO (27.3x forward) and HSY (18.1x), though dearer than KHC and GIS **[VERIFIED]** ([KO](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ko/statistics/); [HSY](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/hsy/statistics/)). ### OLLI: unit growth masks negative comps, and a refund carried the quarter

## Tariff refunds and AI capex distort the headlines

**Three of the nine received IEEPA tariff refunds, and the accounting treatment varies.** OLLI's case is the clearest. The **$28.3M refund added 380 bp to gross margin, more than the total 360 bp gain**, so the underlying gross margin was about 39.7% against 39.9% a year earlier **[VERIFIED refund; PROVISIONAL arithmetic]** ([OLLI 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001639300/000114036126035406/ef20081496_ex99-1.htm)). Excluding the refund, Q2 EPS falls from $1.42 to about $1.07, and the roughly $0.12 raise to FY EPS guidance is smaller than the roughly $0.35 refund. In other words, **the underlying outlook was cut** even though the headline guidance went up **[PROVISIONAL]**. PEP said tariff refunds contributed about **4 points of core operating profit growth** in a quarter where core operating profit grew only 3% **[VERIFIED]** ([PEP 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000077476/000007747626000050/q320268-kxexhibit991.htm)). That implies core operating profit fell about 1% excluding refunds **[PROVISIONAL]**. ZWS treats refunds most conservatively. It excludes them from FCF ($40.4M YTD net) and from adjusted operating income ($49.1M YTD) **[VERIFIED]** ([ZWS 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001439288/000162828026067574/ex991earningsrelease93026.htm)). Even so, YTD GAAP EPS ($1.47) exceeds adjusted EPS ($1.43), so its GAAP figures are the flattered ones. The court ruling behind the refunds (2026-02-20) is sourced only to a third party **[REPORTED]** ([Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/news/ollie-s-bargain-outlet-earnings-2026-annual)).

Other one-offs and distortions go in both directions. GMED's year-ago GAAP EPS included the $110.5M Nevro bargain-purchase gain. ADMA's first-half GAAP net income includes an $8.0M gain from selling plasma centres, and its 2025 GAAP EPS benefited from a low tax rate (14.7% against 24.7% now). ALGN's GAAP operating margin jump from 9.7% to 16.7% mostly reflects lower restructuring charges. MDLZ's GAAP gross margin of +12.6 pp is mark-to-market noise, while its adjusted +380 bp reflects real but cyclical cocoa relief. EME's non-GAAP figures exclude a $34M impairment **[VERIFIED, filings cited above]**. Across all nine, the adjusted figures are the cleaner signal everywhere except OLLI and PEP, where even the "core" or adjusted numbers include the refund.

**AI and data-centre capex is the main shared exposure in the industrials, and it is not evenly distributed.** EME is the most direct: about 47% of RPO and about one-third of revenue. FERG's exposure runs indirectly through non-residential large capital projects (+8%), with a proximity claim that rests on a low-reliability source. ZWS's exposure is **[UNKNOWN]**. Because EME's $18.6B RPO is about 0.9x forward revenue **[PROVISIONAL]**, a hyperscaler slowdown would show up first in **bookings, not revenue**. Hyperscaler capex commentary from MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL and META is therefore a leading indicator for two of the three industrials. Four other patterns cut across sectors **[PROVISIONAL]**. First, ALGN (scanners) and GMED (robots) both report customers moving capital equipment to leases and placements, which depresses system revenue while potentially supporting consumable pull-through. Second, OLLI, ALGN and PEP all cite pressure on lower-income or discretionary consumers. Third, MDLZ and PEP share input-cost risk: cocoa for MDLZ, and hedges rolling off in Q4 for PEP. Fourth, ADMA (debt-funded buybacks), FERG (FloWorks) and EME (about $0.8B of 9M acquisitions plus an impairment) each carry capital-allocation risk.

## Conclusion

The nine names fall into three groups rather than three sectors. In the first group, quality is proven and the debate is about durability: EME, FERG and PEP have ROIC of 18–37%, and the question is whether data-centre demand or North American brand repair holds up. In the second group, quality is improving and is not yet fully reflected in the price: GMED and ZWS, both of which have doubled or rebuilt ROIC while their multiples fell. In the third group, the price is low because one leg of "quality" is genuinely missing: growth for ALGN, predictability and governance for ADMA, and comp momentum for OLLI. MDLZ sits between groups, a fair price for a cyclical input-cost recovery. For a QARP screen, this means a low multiple alone does not distinguish the names. What separates GMED from ALGN, or PEP from OLLI, is whether returns and growth have been improving or deteriorating while the multiple fell.

The next eight days will settle more than the next three months. ADMA (11-04) tests whether a guided second-half ramp of roughly 20–30% sequential is real. GMED (11-05) tests whether integration margins hold while robots and Nevro shrink. FERG (11-09) tests whether non-residential growth can keep offsetting housing, possibly with FloWorks in the numbers for the first time. After that, the calendar goes quiet until the MDLZ, PEP and ALGN guidance resets in late January and early February. Those resets matter more than the quarters themselves, because FY27 guidance is where the tariff-refund boost disappears and where cocoa relief and PepsiCo's price investments either show up in earnings or don't. The OLLI Q3 date, ZWS's end-market mix, FERG's data-centre share, peer multiples for the two healthcare medtechs, and 3- and 6-month returns against the S&P for most names all remain UNKNOWN, and closing those gaps should come before any conclusion is drawn.
