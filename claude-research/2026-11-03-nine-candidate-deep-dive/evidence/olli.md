## Q2. Latest quarter (Q2 FY2026, ended 2026-08-01): growth split, margins, EPS, inventory, FCF, guidance

### Takeaway
Net sales rose 9.1%, all of it from new stores, because comparable-store sales fell 1.8%. The headline margin and EPS beat came mostly from a one-time IEEPA tariff refund. That refund ($28.3M, about 380 bps of gross margin) is larger than the whole 360 bp gross margin gain, so the underlying gross margin was roughly flat to slightly down. Management cut the full-year sales and comp guidance and raised EPS guidance, mainly because of the refund and more buybacks.

## Q3. One-off benefits: bankrupt-retailer leases, closeout buying, Ollie's Army, trade-down

### Takeaway
Ollie's unit growth accelerated sharply after the Big Lots bankruptcy. It acquired at least 63 former Big Lots leases by February 2025 and opened a record 86 stores in fiscal 2025, which drove 16.6% sales growth. That pace is slowing: 75 openings are planned for fiscal 2026, Q2 openings fell to 15 from 29, and pre-opening expense dropped 42%. Comps went from +5.0% to −1.8%. That pattern is consistent with the liquidation-driven tailwinds (customers taken from closing competitors, plus closeout deal flow) fading. In my sources the company does not quantify any of these benefits.

### Cited Findings
- Ollie's won 8 Big Lots leases at a late-2024 bankruptcy auction, had bought 17 former Big Lots stores by December 2024, and agreed in February 2025 to take 40 more from Gordon Brothers, for a total of 63 former Big Lots leases — [Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/olli-adds-8-big-lots-stores-portfolio-what-it-means-investors); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ollies-bargain-outlet-acquire-40-180509029.html); [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/ollies-bargain-outlet-buys-big-lots-leases-new-ceo-take-reins-february) (2024–25)
- In 2025 management said most new stores that year were former Big Lots sites and were beating expectations — [web search summary of 2025 call commentary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ollies-bargain-outlet-q2-earnings-call-highlights) (2025 statement; flag as older)
- Fiscal 2025: a record 86 openings, ending at 645 stores in 34 states (+15.4%). Net sales rose 16.6% to $2,649.2M and comps rose 3.7%. Q4 comps were +3.6%, driven by both basket and transactions — [Q4 FY25 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639300/000114036126009133/ef20067696_ex99-1.htm) (2026-03-12)
- Ollie's Army had 18.1M members at Q2 FY2026, up 12.7% — [Q2 FY26 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001639300/000114036126035406/ef20081496_ex99-1.htm). It had 17.0M at fiscal 2025 year-end, up 12.1% — [Q4 FY25 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639300/000114036126009133/ef20067696_ex99-1.htm)
- Management cites "strong deal flow" and says consumer pressures "are creating buying opportunities across the closeout market". No specific deals were named — [Q2 FY26 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001639300/000114036126035406/ef20081496_ex99-1.htm)
- On trade-down: lower-income customers are focusing on necessities and shopping less often — [Nasdaq call highlights](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ollies-bargain-outlet-q2-earnings-call-highlights) (Sept 2026)

### Inferences
- The lease windfall is not repeatable on demand. It depends on competitors failing. More distress among discount and specialty retailers could bring new waves of leases and inventory, but the timing is unknowable.
- Loyalty membership (+12.7%) grew faster than comps (−1.8%), which suggests lower spend per member. Membership growth mostly tracks new stores.
- The FY2025 comp of +3.7% probably included some benefit from customers transferring from closed Big Lots stores. The −1.8% comp in Q2 FY2026, which laps a +5.0% quarter, suggests that benefit is fading. This is inference; the company has not quantified it.

## Q8. What would invalidate the QARP thesis

### Takeaway
The thesis rests on three things: double-digit unit growth at good new-store returns, positive comps, and stable margins of about 11–12% on a net-cash balance sheet. The main threats are comps staying negative, weaker returns on new stores (especially former Big Lots sites), and margin erosion once the one-time tariff refund is gone.

### Cited Findings (evidence base)
- Comps went from +5.0% to −1.8%, and FY comp guidance was cut from about +2% to 0–0.5% — [Q2 FY26 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001639300/000114036126035406/ef20081496_ex99-1.htm)
- SG&A deleveraged by 80 bps in Q2 — same source.
- ROIC has been only about 10–11% — [Stockanalysis ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/olli/financials/ratios/)

### Inferences (thesis-breakers to monitor)
1. Comps stay negative into the Q3 and Q4 FY2026 prints. The Q4 FY2025 comp was +3.6%, which sets up a tough comparison. Two or more further negative-comp quarters would break the "comp plus units" algorithm.
2. Underlying gross margin, excluding refunds, falls below about 39.5%, or underlying operating margin falls below about 10%.
3. New-store productivity or pre-opening economics worsen, or the company cuts its fiscal 2027 opening plan below about 10% unit growth. This would signal that the post-Big Lots pipeline has been used up.
4. Closeout supply tightens, for example as retail distress eases and fewer liquidations occur, shown by inventory falling sharply per store or by commentary about weaker deal flow.
5. FCF conversion falls back below about 60% or ROIC below about 9%, which would show growth is destroying value.
6. The company moves from net cash to borrowing for buybacks.
7. Note that a "beat" driven by one-time refunds hides the underlying trend. Investors should judge EPS excluding refunds.

### Gaps
- New-store payback and cash-on-cash return targets were not sourced in this pass (UNKNOWN). The 10-K would have them.
