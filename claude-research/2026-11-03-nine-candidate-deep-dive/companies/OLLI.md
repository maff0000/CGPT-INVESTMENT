## At a glance
- **Thesis in one line:** double-digit unit growth (686 stores toward a ~1,300 long-term target [REPORTED]) on a net-cash balance sheet, plus closeout supply from retail distress.
- **Main catch:** the fiscal Q2 beat was almost entirely a one-off. The $28.3M IEEPA tariff refund added 380 bp to gross margin, more than the whole 360 bp gain; comps were −1.8% and the underlying FY outlook was cut [VERIFIED refund; PROVISIONAL arithmetic].
- **Fading tailwind:** the Big Lots lease windfall is slowing (75 openings planned vs a record 86; Q2 openings 15 vs 29) [VERIFIED].
- **QARP fit [PROVISIONAL]:** weakest of the three consumer names. ROIC ~10–11%; the multiple is near the bottom of its own range, but momentum is poor (52-week −27.7%).

## Thesis-breakers
All [PROVISIONAL]; dates estimated where noted.
1. Comps negative again in fiscal Q3 (report ~Dec 2026) and Q4 (~Mar 2027, lapping +3.6%). Two more negative quarters break the "comps plus units" algorithm.
2. Gross margin excluding refunds below ~39.5%, or underlying operating margin below ~10%.
3. Fiscal 2027 opening plan (expected with Q4 results) below ~10% unit growth, or worse new-store productivity or pre-opening economics.
4. Closeout supply tightens: sharply lower inventory per store or weaker deal-flow commentary.
5. FCF conversion below ~60% or ROIC below ~9%.
6. A move from net cash to borrowing for buybacks.
- Read every "beat" ex-refunds: headline EPS and gross margin include the one-off.
